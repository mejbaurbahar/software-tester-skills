# Usability & UX Testing

## Overview
Usability testing evaluates how intuitive, efficient, and error-tolerant an application is for real human users. It identifies friction points, ambiguous affordances, and confusing error handling.

## Nielsen Norman 10 Usability Heuristics Checklist
1. **Visibility of System Status**:
   - Are loading spinners, progress bars, and operation states clearly communicated?
   - Do long operations provide cancel options and ETA?
2. **Match Between System and Real World**:
   - Are labels, terms, and icons familiar to the target user audience (no developer jargon/stack traces)?
3. **User Control and Freedom**:
   - Is there a clear undo/cancel mechanism for destructive actions (e.g. deletion modal, confirmation dialog)?
   - Can users back out of a multi-step modal without losing uncommitted input unexpectedly?
4. **Consistency and Standards**:
   - Are button styles, icon meanings, and terminology consistent across all screens?
5. **Error Prevention**:
   - Are destructive buttons visually distinct (e.g. red, separated from confirm)?
   - Are sensible constraints applied to input fields before form submission?
6. **Recognition Rather Than Recall**:
   - Are instructions and contextual help visible when needed without requiring memorization?
7. **Flexibility and Efficiency of Use**:
   - Are keyboard shortcuts, tab navigation, and search auto-completes supported?
8. **Aesthetic and Minimalist Design**:
   - Is visual hierarchy clear? Is irrelevant or rarely needed information minimized?
9. **Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors**:
   - Are error messages written in plain language indicating the exact cause and clear next steps?
   - Are specific input fields with errors highlighted inline with clear guidance?
10. **Help and Documentation**:
    - Is contextual help easy to find, searchable, and focused on concrete user tasks?
